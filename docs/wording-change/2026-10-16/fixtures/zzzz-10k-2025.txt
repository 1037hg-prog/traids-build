PART I

Item 1. Business

Sample Widgets Inc designs, assembles and sells widgets. We were organised under the laws of a
state of the United States and we have sold widgets continuously since the company was formed.
Our widgets are sold to distributors, to industrial buyers and, in smaller numbers, directly to
the people who use them. We operate one assembly plant and three regional warehouses.

Our customers are concentrated in the industrial sector. No single customer accounted for more
than ten per cent of our revenue in the year covered by this report.

Item 1A. Risk Factors

Our business depends on the continued demand for widgets. If buyers move to a different product,
or if a competitor sells a widget that is cheaper or better than ours, our revenue would fall and
we might not be able to reduce our costs quickly enough to keep the business profitable.

We buy the alloy our widgets are made from under contracts that run for one year. A sharp rise in
the price of that alloy, or the loss of a supplier, would raise our costs. We do not hedge the
price of the alloy and we have no long term supply agreement in place.

Our assembly plant is a single site. A fire, a flood or a long interruption of power at that site
would stop production until the plant could be repaired or production moved elsewhere.

We now depend on one contract manufacturer in a single foreign country for the housings used in
every widget we assemble. If that manufacturer stopped work, or if the goods it ships to us were
delayed at a border or made more expensive by a tariff, we would not be able to assemble widgets
at our present rate and our revenue would fall until another source could be qualified.

PART II

Item 7. Management's Discussion and Analysis of Financial Condition and Results of Operations

Demand fell sharply during the period. Every regional market shrank, several distributors cancelled
standing orders, and the average selling price of a widget declined as competitors discounted. Gross
margin narrowed because fixed assembly costs were spread over fewer units.

We drew on the revolving facility twice during the period and ended with borrowings outstanding. We
have reduced planned capital spending and suspended hiring. Whether operations will fund the coming
period depends on demand recovering, which we cannot promise.

Item 7A. Quantitative and Qualitative Disclosures About Market Risk

We are exposed to changes in the price of the alloy used in our widgets and, through the contract
manufacturer described above, to movements in one foreign currency. We hold no derivative
instruments of any kind.
